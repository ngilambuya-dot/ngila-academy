# Solving Linear Equations by Elimination

## 🎯 What is the Elimination Method?

The **elimination method** (also called the addition method) involves adding or subtracting equations to eliminate one variable. The goal is to get a single equation with one variable.

### The Key Idea:
If a + b = c
and d + b = e
Then (a - d) = (c - e)

text

---

## 📚 When to Use Elimination Method

Use elimination when:
- Both equations are in standard form (`ax + by = c`)
- Coefficients of one variable are opposites
- You can easily multiply to make coefficients match

---

## 🔑 The Elimination Method Steps

### Step-by-Step Process:

**Step 1:** Write equations in standard form: `ax + by = c`
**Step 2:** Multiply equations (if needed) to make coefficients match or be opposites
**Step 3:** Add or subtract equations to eliminate one variable
**Step 4:** Solve for the remaining variable
**Step 5:** Substitute back to find the other variable
**Step 6:** Check your solution in both equations

---

## ✍️ Worked Examples

### Example 1: Adding to Eliminate

**Problem:** Solve the system:
2x + 3y = 11
2x - 3y = 5

text

**Solution:**

**Step 1:** Equations are already in standard form.

**Step 2:** The y-coefficients are opposites (+3 and -3). Add the equations:
2x + 3y = 11
2x - 3y = 5
-------------- Add
4x + 0y = 16

text

**Step 3:** Solve for x:
4x = 16
x = 4

text

**Step 4:** Substitute x = 4 into first equation:
2(4) + 3y = 11
8 + 3y = 11
3y = 3
y = 1

text

**Step 5:** Check:
Equation 1: 2(4) + 3(1) = 8 + 3 = 11 ✅
Equation 2: 2(4) - 3(1) = 8 - 3 = 5 ✅

text

**Answer:** `x = 4, y = 1`

---

### Example 2: Subtracting to Eliminate

**Problem:** Solve the system:
5x + 2y = 19
5x - y = 13

text

**Solution:**

**Step 1:** Equations are in standard form.

**Step 2:** The x-coefficients are the same (5). Subtract the equations:
5x + 2y = 19
5x - y = 13
-------------- Subtract
0x + 3y = 6

text

**Step 3:** Solve for y:
3y = 6
y = 2

text

**Step 4:** Substitute y = 2 into first equation:
5x + 2(2) = 19
5x + 4 = 19
5x = 15
x = 3

text

**Step 5:** Check:
Equation 1: 5(3) + 2(2) = 15 + 4 = 19 ✅
Equation 2: 5(3) - 2 = 15 - 2 = 13 ✅

text

**Answer:** `x = 3, y = 2`

---

### Example 3: Multiplying One Equation

**Problem:** Solve the system:
3x + 2y = 12
x - y = 4

text

**Solution:**

**Step 1:** Equations are in standard form.

**Step 2:** Multiply the second equation by 2 to make y-coefficients opposites:
3x + 2y = 12
2(x - y) = 2(4)
2x - 2y = 8

text

**Step 3:** Add the equations:
3x + 2y = 12
2x - 2y = 8
-------------- Add
5x + 0y = 20

text

**Step 4:** Solve for x:
5x = 20
x = 4

text

**Step 5:** Substitute x = 4 into second original equation:
4 - y = 4
-y = 0
y = 0

text

**Step 6:** Check:
Equation 1: 3(4) + 2(0) = 12 ✅
Equation 2: 4 - 0 = 4 ✅

text

**Answer:** `x = 4, y = 0`

---

### Example 4: Multiplying Both Equations

**Problem:** Solve the system:
2x + 3y = 8
3x + 4y = 11

text

**Solution:**

**Step 1:** Equations are in standard form.

**Step 2:** Multiply first equation by 3, second by 2 to make x-coefficients match:
3(2x + 3y) = 3(8) → 6x + 9y = 24
2(3x + 4y) = 2(11) → 6x + 8y = 22

text

**Step 3:** Subtract the equations:
6x + 9y = 24
6x + 8y = 22
-------------- Subtract
0x + y = 2
y = 2

text

**Step 4:** Substitute y = 2 into first original equation:
2x + 3(2) = 8
2x + 6 = 8
2x = 2
x = 1

text

**Step 5:** Check:
Equation 1: 2(1) + 3(2) = 2 + 6 = 8 ✅
Equation 2: 3(1) + 4(2) = 3 + 8 = 11 ✅

text

**Answer:** `x = 1, y = 2`

---

### Example 5: Elimination with No Solution

**Problem:** Solve the system:
2x + 3y = 7
4x + 6y = 10

text

**Solution:**

**Step 1:** Multiply first equation by 2:
4x + 6y = 14
4x + 6y = 10

text

**Step 2:** Subtract:
0 = 4

text
This is FALSE!

**Answer:** No solution (parallel lines)

---

### Example 6: Elimination with Infinite Solutions

**Problem:** Solve the system:
2x + 3y = 7
4x + 6y = 14

text

**Solution:**

**Step 1:** Multiply first equation by 2:
4x + 6y = 14
4x + 6y = 14

text

**Step 2:** Subtract:
0 = 0

text
This is TRUE for all values!

**Answer:** Infinite solutions (same line)

## 🌍 Real-Life Applications

### Application 1: Ticket Sales

**Problem:** A theater sells adult tickets for $10 and child tickets for $6. On a night, 200 people attended and the total was $1600. How many of each ticket were sold?

**Solution:**
Let `a` = adult tickets, `c` = child tickets
a + c = 200 (total tickets)
10a + 6c = 1600 (total money)

text

**Step 1:** Multiply first equation by -6:
-6a - 6c = -1200
10a + 6c = 1600

text

**Step 2:** Add:
4a = 400
a = 100

text

**Step 3:** Find c:
100 + c = 200
c = 100

text

**Answer:** 100 adult tickets, 100 child tickets

---

### Application 2: Investment Problem

**Problem:** You invest $5000 in two accounts. One pays 5% interest, the other pays 8%. The total interest after one year is $340. How much is in each account?

**Solution:**
Let `x` = amount at 5%, `y` = amount at 8%
x + y = 5000
0.05x + 0.08y = 340

text

**Step 1:** Multiply first by -0.05:
-0.05x - 0.05y = -250
0.05x + 0.08y = 340

text

**Step 2:** Add:
0.03y = 90
y = 3000

text

**Step 3:** Find x:
x + 3000 = 5000
x = 2000

text

**Answer:** $2000 at 5%, $3000 at 8%

---

### Application 3: Speed Problem

**Problem:** A boat travels 120 km downstream in 4 hours and 120 km upstream in 6 hours. Find the boat's speed in still water and the current speed.

**Solution:**
Let `b` = boat speed, `c` = current speed
b + c = 30 (downstream: 120/4 = 30 km/h)
b - c = 20 (upstream: 120/6 = 20 km/h)

text

**Step 1:** Add equations:
2b = 50
b = 25

text

**Step 2:** Find c:
25 + c = 30
c = 5

text

**Answer:** Boat speed = 25 km/h, Current = 5 km/h

## 🚨 Common Mistakes

### Mistake 1: Forgetting to Multiply Both Sides

| ❌ WRONG | ✅ CORRECT |
|----------|-----------|
| `3x + 2y = 12` → multiply by 2: `6x + 4y = 12` | `3x + 2y = 12` → multiply by 2: `6x + 4y = 24` |

**Remember:** Multiply EVERY term on BOTH sides!

---

### Mistake 2: Adding Instead of Subtracting

Always check if coefficients match or are opposites before adding/subtracting.

---

## 📊 Summary

### Steps for Elimination:
1. Write equations in standard form
2. Multiply equations (if needed)
3. Add or subtract to eliminate a variable
4. Solve for the remaining variable
5. Substitute back to find the other variable
6. Check in BOTH equations

### When to Use:
- **Elimination:** Standard form equations
- **Substitution:** One variable already isolated
- **Graphing:** Quick visual estimate
