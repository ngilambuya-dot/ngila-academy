# Solving Linear Equations by Graphing

## 🎯 What is Graphing Method?

The **graphing method** involves drawing both equations on the same coordinate plane. The solution is where the lines intersect.

### The Key Idea:
The point where lines cross is the solution!

text

---

## 📚 When to Use Graphing Method

Use graphing when:
- You need a visual representation
- You need an approximate solution
- You want to check your answer from other methods
- Both equations are in slope-intercept form

---

## 🔑 Review: Graphing Linear Equations

### Slope-Intercept Form:
y = mx + b

text
- `m` = slope (rise/run)
- `b` = y-intercept (where line crosses y-axis)

### Steps to Graph:
1. Plot the y-intercept (0, b)
2. Use slope to find another point
3. Draw the line through both points

---

## ✍️ Worked Examples

### Example 1: Simple Graphing

**Problem:** Solve by graphing:
y = 2x + 1
y = -x + 4

text

**Solution:**

**Step 1:** Graph y = 2x + 1
- y-intercept: (0, 1)
- Slope: 2 (up 2, right 1)

**Step 2:** Graph y = -x + 4
- y-intercept: (0, 4)
- Slope: -1 (down 1, right 1)

**Step 3:** Find intersection point
- The lines cross at (1, 3)

**Step 4:** Check:
Equation 1: 3 = 2(1) + 1 = 3 ✅
Equation 2: 3 = -1 + 4 = 3 ✅

text

**Answer:** `x = 1, y = 3`

---

### Example 2: Graphing with Fractions

**Problem:** Solve by graphing:
y = (1/2)x + 2
y = (-1/2)x + 4

text

**Solution:**

**Step 1:** Graph y = (1/2)x + 2
- y-intercept: (0, 2)
- Slope: 1/2 (up 1, right 2)

**Step 2:** Graph y = (-1/2)x + 4
- y-intercept: (0, 4)
- Slope: -1/2 (down 1, right 2)

**Step 3:** Find intersection point
- The lines cross at (2, 3)

**Step 4:** Check:
Equation 1: 3 = (1/2)(2) + 2 = 3 ✅
Equation 2: 3 = (-1/2)(2) + 4 = 3 ✅

text

**Answer:** `x = 2, y = 3`

---

### Example 3: Graphing with Negative Slope

**Problem:** Solve by graphing:
y = -2x + 5
y = x - 1

text

**Solution:**

**Step 1:** Graph y = -2x + 5
- y-intercept: (0, 5)
- Slope: -2 (down 2, right 1)

**Step 2:** Graph y = x - 1
- y-intercept: (0, -1)
- Slope: 1 (up 1, right 1)

**Step 3:** Find intersection point
- The lines cross at (2, 1)

**Step 4:** Check:
Equation 1: 1 = -2(2) + 5 = 1 ✅
Equation 2: 1 = 2 - 1 = 1 ✅

text

**Answer:** `x = 2, y = 1`

---

### Example 4: Parallel Lines (No Solution)

**Problem:** Solve by graphing:
y = 2x + 3
y = 2x - 2

text

**Solution:**

**Step 1:** Graph y = 2x + 3
- y-intercept: (0, 3)
- Slope: 2

**Step 2:** Graph y = 2x - 2
- y-intercept: (0, -2)
- Slope: 2

**Step 3:** Observe the graphs
- Lines are parallel (same slope, different intercepts)
- They never intersect

**Answer:** No solution

---

### Example 5: Same Line (Infinite Solutions)

**Problem:** Solve by graphing:
y = 3x + 2
6x - 2y = -4

text

**Solution:**

**Step 1:** First equation: y = 3x + 2

**Step 2:** Convert second to slope-intercept:
6x - 2y = -4
-2y = -6x - 4
y = 3x + 2

text

**Step 3:** Both equations are the SAME line!

**Answer:** Infinite solutions

## 🌍 Real-Life Applications

### Application: Break-Even Analysis

**Problem:** A company sells products for $20 each. Fixed costs are $1000, variable costs are $10 per item. Find the break-even point.

**Solution:**
Revenue: R = 20x
Cost: C = 10x + 1000
Break-even: R = C

text

**Step 1:** Graph both lines
- R = 20x (goes through origin, slope 20)
- C = 10x + 1000 (y-intercept 1000, slope 10)

**Step 2:** Find intersection
- Lines cross at x = 100, y = 2000

**Answer:** Break-even at 100 items, $2000 revenue

## 📊 Comparison of Methods

| Method | When to Use | Pros | Cons |
|--------|-------------|------|------|
| **Substitution** | One variable isolated | Exact answer | Can be messy |
| **Elimination** | Standard form equations | Clean, systematic | More steps |
| **Graphing** | Need visual check | Visual understanding | Approximate sometimes |

## 🚨 Common Mistakes

### Mistake 1: Incorrect Slope

| ❌ WRONG | ✅ CORRECT |
|----------|-----------|
| Slope = 2 means up 2, left 1 | Slope = 2 means up 2, RIGHT 1 |

**Remember:** Slope = Rise/Run (run goes RIGHT)

---

### Mistake 2: Wrong y-intercept

| ❌ WRONG | ✅ CORRECT |
|----------|-----------|
| y = 2x + 3 → (3, 0) | y = 2x + 3 → (0, 3) |

**Remember:** y-intercept is where x = 0

## 📊 Summary

### Graphing Steps:
1. Put equations in slope-intercept form
2. Plot y-intercepts
3. Use slope to find another point
4. Draw the lines
5. Find intersection point
6. Check the solution

### Types of Solutions:
- **One solution** → Lines intersect at one point
- **No solution** → Lines are parallel
- **Infinite solutions** → Lines are the same
