# Logarithms

## 🎯 What are Logarithms?

A **logarithm** is the inverse of an exponent. If a power is the answer to "what is the result?", a logarithm asks "what is the index?"

### The Relationship:
If 2³ = 8
Then log₂8 = 3 (read as "log base 2 of 8 equals 3")

text

### General Form:
If aˣ = N
Then logₐN = x

text

---

## 📊 Parts of a Logarithm
log₂8 = 3
↑ ↑ ↑
Base Argument Value

text

- **Base (2):** The number being multiplied
- **Argument (8):** The result
- **Value (3):** The power/index

---

## 🔑 Common Logarithms

### Base 10 Logarithms
Also called "common logarithms." Written as `log` without a base.
log₁₀ 100 = 2 (since 10² = 100)
log 1000 = 3 (since 10³ = 1000)

text

### Natural Logarithms
Base `e` (≈ 2.718). Written as `ln`.
ln(e) = 1
ln(1) = 0

text

---

## 📊 Laws of Logarithms

### Law 1: Product Law
The logarithm of a product is the sum of the logarithms.
logₐ(MN) = logₐM + logₐN

text

**Example:**
log₂(4 × 8) = log₂4 + log₂8 = 2 + 3 = 5

text

---

### Law 2: Quotient Law
The logarithm of a quotient is the difference of the logarithms.
logₐ(M/N) = logₐM - logₐN

text

**Example:**
log₂(8/4) = log₂8 - log₂4 = 3 - 2 = 1

text

---

### Law 3: Power Law
The logarithm of a power is the exponent times the logarithm.
logₐ(Mⁿ) = n × logₐM

text

**Example:**
log₂(4³) = 3 × log₂4 = 3 × 2 = 6

text

---

### Law 4: Change of Base
logₐN = log_bN / log_ba

text

**Example:**
log₃7 = log₁₀7 / log₁₀3 ≈ 1.771

text

## 🔢 Working with Logarithms

### Example 1: Simple Evaluation

**Problem:** Evaluate log₂16

**Solution:**
2⁴ = 16
log₂16 = 4

text

---

### Example 2: Product Law

**Problem:** Simplify log₃9 + log₃27

**Solution:**
log₃9 = 2 (since 3² = 9)
log₃27 = 3 (since 3³ = 27)
2 + 3 = 5

text

---

### Example 3: Quotient Law

**Problem:** Simplify log₃27 - log₃9

**Solution:**
log₃27 = 3
log₃9 = 2
3 - 2 = 1

text

---

### Example 4: Power Law

**Problem:** Simplify log₂16³

**Solution:**
log₂16³ = 3 × log₂16 = 3 × 4 = 12

text

---

### Example 5: Solving Logarithmic Equations

**Problem:** Solve log₂x = 5

**Solution:**
log₂x = 5
x = 2⁵ = 32

text

---

### Example 6: Change of Base

**Problem:** Evaluate log₄8

**Solution:**
log₄8 = log₂8 / log₂4 = 3/2 = 1.5

text

## 🌍 Real-Life Applications

### Application 1: Earthquake Magnitude

The Richter scale uses logarithms.
Magnitude = log₁₀(Intensity)

text

### Application 2: Sound Volume

Decibels (dB) are measured on a logarithmic scale.
dB = 10 × log₁₀(Intensity/Reference)

text

### Application 3: pH Scale

pH measures acidity using logarithms.
pH = -log₁₀[H⁺]

text

### Application 4: Population Growth
P = P₀(1 + r)ᵗ
log(P) = log(P₀) + t × log(1 + r)

text

## 🚨 Common Mistakes

### Mistake 1: Forgetting the Base

| ❌ WRONG | ✅ CORRECT |
|----------|-----------|
| log100 = 2 (if base is unspecified, it's 10) | log₁₀100 = 2 |

---

### Mistake 2: Confusing log(a+b) with log(a) + log(b)

| ❌ WRONG | ✅ CORRECT |
|----------|-----------|
| log(a + b) = log a + log b | log(a × b) = log a + log b |

## 📊 Summary

### Key Points:

1. **Logarithms** are the inverse of indices
2. **Base** is the number being multiplied
3. **Argument** is the result
4. **Value** is the index

### Laws Summary:

| Law | Formula |
|-----|---------|
| Product | logₐ(MN) = logₐM + logₐN |
| Quotient | logₐ(M/N) = logₐM - logₐN |
| Power | logₐ(Mⁿ) = n × logₐM |
| Change of Base | logₐN = log_bN / log_ba |
