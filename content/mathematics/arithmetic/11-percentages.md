# Percentages

## 🎯 What is a Percentage?

A **percentage** means "out of 100". The symbol % means percent.
45% = 45/100 = 0.45

text

**The Formula:**
Percentage = (Part / Whole) × 100

text

---

## 🔄 Converting Between Forms

### Percentage → Decimal
Divide by 100
45% = 45 ÷ 100 = 0.45

text

### Percentage → Fraction
Write over 100 and simplify
45% = 45/100 = 9/20

text

### Decimal → Percentage
Multiply by 100
0.45 = 0.45 × 100 = 45%

text

### Fraction → Percentage
Divide numerator by denominator, multiply by 100
3/4 = 0.75 × 100 = 75%

text

---

## 📊 Finding Percentage of a Quantity

### Formula: Percentage × Quantity

### Example 1: Finding Percentage

**Problem:** What is 25% of 200?

**Solution:**
25% of 200 = 25/100 × 200 = 0.25 × 200 = 50

text

---

### Example 2: Real-Life

**Problem:** A shirt costs $80 and is on 15% discount. What is the discount amount?

**Solution:**
15% of 80 = 0.15 × 80 = $12

text

---

### Example 3: Finding Percentage

**Problem:** What percentage of 80 is 20?

**Solution:**
Percentage = (20/80) × 100 = 0.25 × 100 = 25%

text

---

## 📊 Percentage Increase and Decrease

### Percentage Increase:
Increase = New - Original
Percentage Increase = (Increase / Original) × 100

text

### Percentage Decrease:
Decrease = Original - New
Percentage Decrease = (Decrease / Original) × 100

text

---

### Example 4: Percentage Increase

**Problem:** A price increased from $50 to $60. What is the percentage increase?

**Solution:**
Increase = $60 - $50 = $10
Percentage Increase = (10/50) × 100 = 20%

text

---

### Example 5: Percentage Decrease

**Problem:** A price decreased from $60 to $50. What is the percentage decrease?

**Solution:**
Decrease = $60 - $50 = $10
Percentage Decrease = (10/60) × 100 = 16.67%

text

---

## 💰 Profit and Loss

### Profit:
Profit = Selling Price - Cost Price
Profit Percentage = (Profit / Cost Price) × 100

text

### Loss:
Loss = Cost Price - Selling Price
Loss Percentage = (Loss / Cost Price) × 100

text

---

### Example 6: Profit

**Problem:** A trader buys an item for $50 and sells it for $65. What is the profit percentage?

**Solution:**
Profit = $65 - $50 = $15
Profit Percentage = (15/50) × 100 = 30%

text

---

### Example 7: Loss

**Problem:** A trader buys an item for $60 and sells it for $50. What is the loss percentage?

**Solution:**
Loss = $60 - $50 = $10
Loss Percentage = (10/60) × 100 = 16.67%

text

---

## 🛍️ Discount and Mark-up

### Discount:
Discount = Marked Price × Discount Rate
Sale Price = Marked Price - Discount

text

### Mark-up:
Mark-up = Cost Price × Mark-up Rate
Selling Price = Cost Price + Mark-up

text

---

### Example 8: Discount

**Problem:** A television costs $500. It is on sale at 20% discount. What is the sale price?

**Solution:**
Discount = 20% of $500 = 0.20 × 500 = $100
Sale Price = $500 - $100 = $400

text

---

### Example 9: Mark-up

**Problem:** A shopkeeper buys a shirt for $40 and marks it up by 30%. What is the selling price?

**Solution:**
Mark-up = 30% of $40 = 0.30 × 40 = $12
Selling Price = $40 + $12 = $52

text

## 🌍 Real-Life Applications

### Application 1: Tax

> You buy a laptop for $800 and pay 15% VAT.
>
> **Tax:** 15% of $800 = 0.15 × 800 = $120
> **Total:** $800 + $120 = $920

### Application 2: Commission

> A salesperson earns 10% commission on sales. If sales are $10,000, how much commission?
>
> **Commission:** 10% of $10,000 = $1,000

### Application 3: Population Growth

> A town's population increased from 5,000 to 5,500.
>
> **Increase:** 5,500 - 5,000 = 500
> **Percentage Increase:** (500/5,000) × 100 = 10%

## 🚨 Common Mistakes

### Mistake 1: Confusing Increase and Decrease

| ❌ WRONG | ✅ CORRECT |
|----------|-----------|
| 50 to 60: 20% decrease | 50 to 60: 20% increase |

---

### Mistake 2: Calculating on Wrong Base

| ❌ WRONG | ✅ CORRECT |
|----------|-----------|
| 60 to 50: 20% decrease using 50 | 60 to 50: 16.67% decrease using 60 |

## 📊 Summary

### Key Points:

1. **Percentage** = out of 100
2. **Formula:** Percentage = (Part/Whole) × 100
3. **Percentage increase:** (New - Original)/Original × 100
4. **Percentage decrease:** (Original - New)/Original × 100
5. **Profit:** Selling Price - Cost Price
6. **Loss:** Cost Price - Selling Price
7. **Discount:** Marked Price - Sale Price
