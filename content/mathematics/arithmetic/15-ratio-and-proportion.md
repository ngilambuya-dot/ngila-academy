# Ratio and Proportion

## 🎯 What is a Ratio?

A **ratio** compares two or more quantities of the same kind. It shows the relative sizes of different parts.

### Notation:
3:5 (read as "3 to 5")

text

This means for every 3 of one thing, there are 5 of another.

---

## 📊 Writing Ratios

### Example 1: Simple Ratio

**Problem:** A class has 12 boys and 8 girls. What is the ratio of boys to girls?

**Solution:**
Boys : Girls = 12 : 8
Simplify: 3 : 2

text

---

### Example 2: Ratio in Simplest Form

**Problem:** Express 15:20 in simplest form

**Solution:**
15:20 = 15 ÷ 5 : 20 ÷ 5 = 3:4

text

---

### Example 3: Ratio of Three Quantities

**Problem:** John has 3 apples, Mary has 5, Peter has 2. Ratio?

**Solution:**
John : Mary : Peter = 3 : 5 : 2

text

---

## 🔢 Equivalent Ratios

Equivalent ratios are like equivalent fractions – they represent the same relationship.

### Rule: Multiply or divide both terms by the same number
2:3 = 4:6 = 6:9 = 8:12

text

**Example:**
1:2 = 3:6 = 5:10 = 10:20

text

---

## 📊 Sharing in a Given Ratio

### Steps:
1. Find total number of parts
2. Find value of one part
3. Multiply by the number of parts for each share

---

### Example 4: Sharing in a Ratio

**Problem:** Share $100 between John and Mary in the ratio 3:2

**Solution:**
Total parts = 3 + 2 = 5
Value of 1 part = $100 ÷ 5 = $20

John (3 parts) = 3 × $20 = $60
Mary (2 parts) = 2 × $20 = $40

Answer: John gets $60, Mary gets $40

text

---

### Example 5: Three-Part Ratio

**Problem:** Share $120 among John, Mary, and Peter in the ratio 3:2:1

**Solution:**
Total parts = 3 + 2 + 1 = 6
Value of 1 part = $120 ÷ 6 = $20

John (3 parts) = $60
Mary (2 parts) = $40
Peter (1 part) = $20

text

## 🔗 Proportions

A **proportion** states that two ratios are equal.
3:4 = 6:8

text

### Cross Multiplication:
If a:b = c:d
Then a × d = b × c

text

---

### Example 6: Solving Proportions

**Problem:** If 3:4 = x:8, find x

**Solution:**
3/4 = x/8
3 × 8 = 4 × x
24 = 4x
x = 6

text

---

## 📊 Direct Proportion

Two quantities are in **direct proportion** when they increase or decrease together at the same rate.
y ∝ x
y = kx (where k is the constant of proportionality)

text

**Example:** The cost of apples is directly proportional to the number bought.
- 2 apples cost $4
- 4 apples cost $8

---

### Example 7: Direct Proportion

**Problem:** If 5 books cost $20, how much do 8 books cost?

**Solution:**
5 books → $20
1 book → $4 (direct proportion)
8 books → 8 × $4 = $32

text

---

## 📊 Inverse Proportion

Two quantities are in **inverse proportion** when one increases as the other decreases.
y ∝ 1/x
y = k/x (where k is the constant)

text

**Example:** The time taken to travel a distance is inversely proportional to speed.
- 60 km/h → 2 hours
- 120 km/h → 1 hour

---

### Example 8: Inverse Proportion

**Problem:** 6 workers can paint a house in 8 days. How long would it take 4 workers?

**Solution:**
6 × 8 = 48 (total worker-days)
4 workers → 48 ÷ 4 = 12 days

text

## 🌍 Real-Life Applications

### Application 1: Recipe Scaling

> A recipe for 4 people needs 2 cups of flour. How much flour for 6 people?
>
> **Ratio:** 4:6 = 2:x
> **Solution:** x = 3 cups

### Application 2: Map Scale

> A map has scale 1:50,000. How far is 5 cm on the map in reality?
>
> **Solution:** 5 × 50,000 cm = 250,000 cm = 2.5 km

### Application 3: Speed and Time

> At 60 km/h, a trip takes 4 hours. At 80 km/h, how long?
>
> **Inverse proportion:** 60 × 4 = 240 = 80 × t, t = 3 hours
