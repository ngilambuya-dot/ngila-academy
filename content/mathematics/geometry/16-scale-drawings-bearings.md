Scale Drawings and Bearings
🎯 What are Scale Drawings?
A scale drawing is a representation of an object that is smaller or larger than the actual object.

Key Concept:
Scale drawings use a ratio to relate drawing size to actual size.

Scale Notation:
text
1 : 100
(Read as "1 unit on the drawing represents 100 units in real life")

📊 Scale Drawings
Types of Scales:
Scale	Meaning	Example
1 : 100	1 cm = 1 m	Map
1 : 50,000	1 cm = 500 m	Ordnance Survey map
1 : 1,000,000	1 cm = 10 km	Road map
2 : 1	2 cm = 1 cm	Magnification
Converting Scales:
Map scale: 1 : 50,000

Map Distance	Actual Distance
1 cm	50,000 cm = 500 m
5 cm	250,000 cm = 2.5 km
10 cm	500,000 cm = 5 km
Example 1: Calculating Actual Distance
Problem: On a map with scale 1 : 10,000, two points are 5 cm apart. What is the actual distance?

Solution:

text
1 cm = 10,000 cm = 100 m
5 cm = 5 × 100 m = 500 m
Example 2: Calculating Scale
Problem: An object is 20 m long. On a drawing it is 5 cm long. Find the scale.

Solution:

text
5 cm = 20 m
1 cm = 4 m = 400 cm
Scale = 1 : 400
🎯 What are Bearings?
A bearing is a direction measured in degrees, measured clockwise from North.

Key Concept:
Bearings are always measured clockwise from North.

Rules:
✅ Always 3 digits (e.g., 045°, 180°, 270°)

✅ Measured clockwise from North

✅ Written as three figures

text
        North (000°)
            ↑
            │
            │
    270° ←──┼──→ 090°
            │
            │
            ↓
        South (180°)
📊 Three-Figure Bearings
Examples:
Direction	Bearing
North	000°
East	090°
South	180°
West	270°
Northeast	045°
Southeast	135°
Southwest	225°
Northwest	315°
📊 Bearings from a Point
How to Find a Bearing:
text
Step 1: Draw North from the starting point
Step 2: Measure the angle clockwise from North
Step 3: Write as three figures
text
Example: Bearing of B from A is 060°

        N
        ↑
        │  60°
        └───────→ B
        A
📊 Back Bearings
Back bearing is the bearing from the destination back to the start.

Rule:
Back bearing = Forward bearing ± 180°

Formula:
text
If forward bearing < 180°: Back bearing = forward bearing + 180°
If forward bearing > 180°: Back bearing = forward bearing - 180°
Examples:
Forward Bearing	Back Bearing
045°	225°
090°	270°
180°	000°
270°	090°
300°	120°
✍️ Worked Examples
Example 3: Finding a Bearing
Problem: From point A, point B is northeast. What is the bearing of B from A?

Solution:

text
Northeast is halfway between North and East
North = 000°, East = 090°
Halfway = (0 + 90) / 2 = 45°
Bearing = 045°
Example 4: Finding Back Bearing
Problem: The bearing of B from A is 120°. What is the bearing of A from B?

Solution:

text
Back bearing = 120° + 180° = 300°
Example 5: Bearing from Coordinates
Problem: A is at (2,3) and B is at (5,7). Find the bearing of B from A.

Solution:

text
Step 1: Find the difference in coordinates
dx = 5 - 2 = 3
dy = 7 - 3 = 4

Step 2: Find the angle
tan θ = dy/dx = 4/3
θ = tan⁻¹(4/3) = 53.13°

Bearing = 053° (to nearest degree)
Example 6: Scale Drawing with Bearings
Problem: On a map, a ship sails from A to B. B is 6 km from A on a bearing of 060°. Draw the journey.

Solution:

text
Scale: 1 cm = 2 km
6 km = 3 cm on map

Draw:
1. Draw North line at A
2. Measure 60° clockwise
3. Draw a line 3 cm long
4. Mark point B

Application: Map making.
