# Introduction to Complex Numbers

## 🎯 What is a Complex Number?

A **complex number** is a number that can be written in the form:
z = a + bi

text

Where:
- **a** and **b** are **real numbers** (a ∈ ℝ, b ∈ ℝ)
- **i** is the **imaginary unit** (i² = -1)
- **a** is the **real part**
- **b** is the **imaginary part**

### Key Concept:
> **Every complex number has two parts: a real part and an imaginary part.**

---

## 📊 Why Do We Need Complex Numbers?

### The Problem:
x² = -1

text
There is no real number that satisfies this equation because:
- 1² = 1
- (-1)² = 1
- No real number squared gives -1

### The Solution:
> **We define i = √(-1), so i² = -1**

This is the **imaginary unit** that allows us to solve equations like x² + 1 = 0.

---

## 📊 The Imaginary Unit i

### Definition:
i = √(-1)
i² = -1

text

### Powers of i:

| Power | Value | Pattern |
|-------|-------|---------|
| i¹ | i | - |
| i² | -1 | - |
| i³ | -i | i² × i = -1 × i = -i |
| i⁴ | 1 | i² × i² = (-1) × (-1) = 1 |
| i⁵ | i | (Pattern repeats every 4) |

### The Pattern:
i⁴ = 1
i⁵ = i
i⁶ = -1
i⁷ = -i
i⁸ = 1
... and so on

text

### Example 1: Simplifying Powers of i

**Problem:** Simplify i⁷

**Solution:**
i⁷ = i⁴ × i³ = 1 × (-i) = -i

text

## 📊 Standard Form: a + bi

### Real and Imaginary Parts:
For z = a + bi:

Re(z) = a (the real part)

Im(z) = b (the imaginary part)

text

### Examples:

| Complex Number | Real Part (a) | Imaginary Part (b) |
|----------------|---------------|-------------------|
| 3 + 4i | 3 | 4 |
| 5 - 2i | 5 | -2 |
| 7i | 0 | 7 |
| 6 | 6 | 0 |
| -3 + 2i | -3 | 2 |

---

## 📊 Types of Complex Numbers

### 1. Purely Real Numbers
Numbers with no imaginary part (b = 0)
z = a (where a ∈ ℝ)
Example: 5 = 5 + 0i

text

### 2. Purely Imaginary Numbers
Numbers with no real part (a = 0)
z = bi (where b ∈ ℝ)
Example: 3i = 0 + 3i

text

### 3. General Complex Numbers
Numbers with both real and imaginary parts
z = a + bi (where a, b ∈ ℝ, b ≠ 0)
Example: 3 + 4i

text

---

## 📊 Equality of Complex Numbers

### Rule:
> **Two complex numbers are equal if and only if their real parts are equal AND their imaginary parts are equal.**
If a + bi = c + di
Then a = c AND b = d

text

### Example 2: Finding Unknowns

**Problem:** Find x and y if x + yi = 5 + 3i

**Solution:**
x = 5 (real parts equal)
y = 3 (imaginary parts equal)

text

---

## 📊 Complex Conjugate

### Definition:
> **The complex conjugate of z = a + bi is z̄ = a - bi**

### Notation:
z̄ (read as "z bar")

text

### Properties:

| Property | Example |
|----------|---------|
| z + z̄ = 2a | (3+4i) + (3-4i) = 6 |
| z - z̄ = 2bi | (3+4i) - (3-4i) = 8i |
| z × z̄ = a² + b² | (3+4i)(3-4i) = 9 + 16 = 25 |

---

## ✍️ Worked Examples

### Example 3: Identifying Parts

**Problem:** Identify the real and imaginary parts of z = -4 + 7i

**Solution:**
Re(z) = -4
Im(z) = 7

text

---

### Example 4: Simplifying i Powers

**Problem:** Simplify i²⁵

**Solution:**
Step 1: Divide 25 by 4
25 ÷ 4 = 6 remainder 1

Step 2: i²⁵ = i¹ = i

text

---

### Example 5: Equality

**Problem:** Find x and y if x + yi = 7 - 2i

**Solution:**
x = 7
y = -2

text

## 🌍 Real-Life Applications

### Application 1: Engineering

> Complex numbers are used in electrical engineering for AC circuits.
>
> **Application:** Analyzing impedance and phase.

### Application 2: Physics

> Used in quantum mechanics and wave equations.
>
> **Application:** Describing wave functions.

### Application 3: Signal Processing

> Used in audio and image processing.
>
> **Application:** Fourier transforms.

## 📊 Summary

### Key Points:

1. **Complex Number:** z = a + bi (where a, b ∈ ℝ)
2. **Real Part:** Re(z) = a
3. **Imaginary Part:** Im(z) = b
4. **Imaginary Unit:** i² = -1
5. **Conjugate:** z̄ = a - bi

### Important Rules:
i² = -1
i⁴ = 1
z = z̄ → z is real
z = -z̄ → z is purely imaginary
